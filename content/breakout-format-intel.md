## 1. How to read this file

Three labels appear after nearly every claim. They are the whole point of this page:

- **Platform-stated** — YouTube, TikTok, or Meta said it themselves (blog, Creator Insider, newsroom). Strongest signal, but platforms describe intent; the feed implements reality.
- **Verified** — multiple independent sources report the same thing. Strong enough to plan on.
- **Single-source** — one outlet, one creator's dataset, or creator lore. Directional only. If a decision costs real money, find a second source.

Every dossier follows the same seven fields: **the signal** (what the research measured), **the pay** (creator-reported ranges with who reported and when), **the competition** (how crowded the lane is), **what it takes** (entry requirements — gear, skills, startup cost), **who it suits** (the creator it rewards), **the risks** (what can break the case), and **the 2027 read** (where the current signals point — directional, never a prediction).

**The golden rule:** ranges are what the market looked like; they say nothing about what any individual channel will earn. When this page and your analytics disagree, your analytics are right.

**The companion layer:** this file is the deep expansion of [The Niche Atlas](niche-atlas.html) — the atlas carries the ranking tables, the 2027 trajectory badges, the competition-vs-reward scatter, and the niche-to-revenue-stream fit matrix. ## 2. The reality check, restated

**Fewer than 1% of YouTube channels ever reach 100,000 subscribers** (Lilach Bullock's own analysis, updated October 2026 — single-source). Plan your business around 10K–30K loyal subscribers, not a million. The dossiers below are about *formats*, not certainties: a 21x breakout multiple describes what exceptional videos did, not what typical videos do.

And the central trap of the whole page: **growth is not revenue.** Kids content grew the fastest of any niche measured — 1.59% monthly median channel growth — and pays the worst: **$0.33 RPM**, about 3 million views to earn $1,000 (AIR Media-Tech 300-channel dataset, May 2025–May 2026 — verified). Every dossier states its pay range precisely so you can see this trade-off before you pick.

## 3. DOSSIER 01 // Betrayal & revenge mini-dramas

**The signal.** The single strongest breakout multiple in the October 2026 research: **21x** — videos beating their channel's average by 21 times (OutlierKit trending-niches data — single-source). Underneath the multiple, the platform tailwind is real: microdramas passed **6.5 billion views on YouTube in H1 2026**, with watch time up over 50% year over year (AIR Media-Tech — verified outlet). In September 2026, YouTube started letting creators group Shorts into **seasons and episodes** — first-class support for episodic short scripted series with recurring casts (AIR — verified outlet). TikTok has been promoting episodic mini-drama series since June 2026 (buyTikTokAccount — single-source).

**The pay.** **$20–25 CPM**, creator-reported (OutlierKit — single-source). Ranges, never promises: CPM is what advertisers bid for this audience, not what any given channel earns — the site's Platform Rules Bible shows how channel medians sit far below advertiser CPMs.

**The competition.** **Low** (OutlierKit — single-source). The lane rewards original scripted production; volume-farmed versions of it were removed from monetization in January 2026 (see the risks).

**What it takes.** Scripted-drama production: scripts with cliffhangers, a cast (even a small recurring one), basic directing, clean dialogue audio, and editing paced for vertical episodes. AI video generation cuts production cost — 470 AI-generated short dramas were being released per day in China by January 2026 (YourStory — single-source), and by September 2026, **201 of 221** new dramas tracked across ReelShort, DramaBox, ShortMax, and GoodShort were AI-made, up from 0 of 16 in January (GASP public dataset — single-source). A phone, a script, and two actors is a legitimate entry point; a small team with a storyboard is better.

**Who it suits.** Storytellers with drama instincts — writers who understand cliffhangers, melodrama pacing, and serialized payoff. Small production teams over solo operators. Anyone who has ever binge-watched a telenovela and thought "I could structure that" — this format is closer to serialized soap craft than to film school.

**The risks.** The supply-glut counterweight is brutal: on Douyin, 221,900 AI/animated dramas were released in H1 2026 with 515.7 billion views — but only **1 in 77 recouped its investment; 98.7% failed to break even** (The Paper, ~October 5, 2026 — single-source). DataEye's VP put the lesson in one line: "AI is not lowering costs so much as lowering the threshold for losing money." AI volume is the trap; original production is the moat. Two structural risks on top: YouTube's January 2026 inauthentic-content enforcement excluded mass-produced, generic, or repetitive videos from monetization (UTubeKit — verified publication), and from February 1, 2027, Shorts revenue sharing requires **10 million qualified Shorts views per 90 days** (AuditSocials — verified multi-source) — a serialized Shorts drama is Shorts-heavy by construction.

**The 2027 read.** Heating — the platform is betting on the format (YouTube's 2027 priorities include micro-dramas per analyst Lia Haberman's read — single-source), but the read is for *original* scripted series, not AI-volume farms. The evidence bar for a business here is a distinctive voice, a recurring cast, and episodes people actually finish.

## 4. DOSSIER 02 // English-learning podcasts

**The signal.** **21x** breakout multiple, tied with betrayal dramas for the top of the OutlierKit trending-niches board (single-source). The distribution rails are already built: YouTube Podcasts passed **1 billion monthly users** (AIR — verified outlet), and long conversation formats are the ones that travel to TV.

**The pay.** **$18–22 CPM** creator-reported (OutlierKit — single-source); **$11.88 RPM** reported (UTubeKit, July 2026 — verified publication, creator-reported range). One of the rare niches with both a CPM and an RPM figure in the research — read both as ranges.

**The competition.** **Low** (OutlierKit — single-source).

**What it takes.** Teaching ability, clear and warm audio (the product is the voice), and a consistent schedule — this format compounds on cadence, not on individual videos. A decent microphone, a quiet room, and a lesson plan per episode. Video podcasts win over audio-only because YouTube is a discovery surface; the video doesn't need to be cinematic, it needs to be present.

**Who it suits.** Teachers, coaches, and multilingual creators. Anyone who can hold a conversation for 30 minutes and make a listener feel smarter. The format rewards patience and structure over charisma — the audience is students, not fans.

**The risks.** Language-education advertisers are a specific buyer set; if that buyer set pauses, the $18–22 CPM moves with it. The figures are single-source — this is the dossier where a second source matters most before spending real money.

**The 2027 read.** Heating — education-tier rates plus low competition plus AI translation expanding global reach is a three-signal tailwind. The Niche Atlas rates it Heating with the same basis.

## 5. DOSSIER 03 // Senior health

**The signal.** **19x** breakout multiple (OutlierKit — single-source), in the niche the research calls **ultra-low competition** (OutlierKit — single-source). The senior audience is underserved on nearly every platform — this is a demand-side gap, not a supply-side fad.

**The pay.** **No reported range** in the October 2026 research. The honest way to price it: the lifestyle mid-tier proxy (the Niche Atlas places comparable niches at roughly $4–10 RPM, creator-reported — verified publication, directional). No reported range is itself data — treat any figure anyone quotes you as lore until it's sourced.

**The competition.** **Ultra-low** (OutlierKit — single-source). This is the lowest competition figure attached to any measured breakout in the research.

**What it takes.** Accuracy as the brand: health content for an older audience demands correct, clearly explained, well-sourced information — cite your sources on screen. Warm, patient delivery; large-type visuals; slow pacing that respects the viewer. A webcam, a microphone, and rigorous notes are enough; production flash is not the product.

**Who it suits.** Health professionals, caregivers, and patient communicators — nurses, physios, pharmacists, anyone who has explained a treatment plan to a worried family member and been thanked for the clarity. vidIQ's low-competition/high-demand list independently flags **senior digital literacy** — the same audience, adjacent subject (verified publication).

**The risks.** The bright red line: **health education is fine; medical-advice claims are not.** TikTok removes medical-advice content as a category (buyTikTokAccount — single-source), and AI personas giving health advice are unmonetizable (Synthesys — single-source). This site's honesty rule applies double here: educational content only, never diagnoses or treatment recommendations — and nothing on this page is medical advice. Get this wrong and you lose the channel, not just the video.

**The 2027 read.** Heating — the audience is growing, the competition is near-zero, and accuracy is a defensible moat. The Atlas rates it Heating with the same basis.

## 6. DOSSIER 04 // Sleep & ambient

**The signal.** **14x** breakout multiple (OutlierKit — single-source). The quietest format on the board, and one of the steadiest: sleep content compounds on catalog, not on hits — every night is a viewing session.

**The pay.** **Mid-tier**, via the lifestyle proxy (the Niche Atlas places comparable niches at roughly $4–10 RPM, creator-reported — verified publication, directional). Sleep audiences are loyal and long-watching, which is exactly what retention-based monetization rewards.

**The competition.** **Low** (OutlierKit — single-source).

**What it takes.** Audio production quality — the sound *is* the product. Rain, brown noise, ambient drones, lo-fi loops: each needs clean recording, mastering for earbuds and speakers, and hours-long session design. Consistency matters more than novelty; the audience wants the next episode to feel like the last one. A field recorder or a good DAW and a quiet room are the entry kit.

**Who it suits.** Audio engineers, musicians, producers, and calm voices. Anyone who has ever been told "your voice puts me to sleep" — this is the one niche where that's the entire business model.

**The risks.** YouTube's January 2026 inauthentic-content enforcement hit low-effort faceless channels hardest: mass-produced, generic, or repetitive videos were excluded from monetization (UTubeKit — verified publication). The rule for this dossier is the whole thesis: **faceless still works, but only with visible human creativity** — original audio, real production, a human making decisions. A sleep channel that is genuinely produced is safe; a sleep channel that is a render farm is not. The Niche Atlas rates it Stable for exactly this reason.

**The 2027 read.** Stable — 14x breakout against the enforcement overhang. Original production is the entry ticket; the catalog compounds for years once you're in.

## 7. DOSSIER 05 // AI tools & tutorials

**The signal.** The fastest-rising search niche of 2026: **+340% year-over-year search growth** (OutlierKit, September 2026 — verified publication). 10.0% of videos in the niche beat their channel average by 10x (OutlierKit breakout data, August 2026 — verified publication), and AI tutorials were separately measured at **18x growth at $15–22 CPM** (OutlierKit trending — single-source). The channel-growth data agrees: AI channels with 10K–100K subscribers grew **1.44% per month** vs the 0.97% all-channel median (AIR Media-Tech via TubeAI, Sep 7–27 2026 — verified).

**The pay.** **$8–20 CPM** creator-reported (OutlierKit — verified publication), with the $15–22 CPM breakout figure (OutlierKit — single-source) as the high-water mark. Ranges, never promises.

**The competition.** Rising fast — the Atlas's own note: specificity wins. The broad "AI news" lane is saturated; the growing lanes are narrow: local AI tutorials, AI for a specific profession, AI for a specific workflow (vidIQ — verified publication).

**What it takes.** Technical fluency and fast iteration — the tools change monthly, and yesterday's tutorial is today's misinformation. On-screen teaching **in your own voice**: YouTube's inauthentic-content policy explicitly draws the line at mass-produced AI videos while letting AI *tutorials* grow (AIR — verified outlet). Screen recording, a microphone, and the willingness to redo a video when the UI updates.

**Who it suits.** Tech-native experimenters. People who already live in new tools and explain them to friends unprompted. Niche professionals teaching AI to their own industry — the local-AI-tutorial lane vidIQ flags — get the underserved half of the market.

**The risks.** Tool half-lives are the structural risk: your catalog decays as tools change. Disclosure rules are the compliance risk: May-2026 auto-labeling of undisclosed photorealistic AI (AIR — verified), and TikTok's October 2026 AI-transparency push — 3 billion+ videos labeled AI-generated, with AI-spam detection testing (TikTok newsroom — platform-stated). Disclose what the tools do; the policy punishes concealment, not AI use.

**The 2027 read.** Heating — the strongest multi-signal dossier on the page: search growth, breakout rate, channel growth, and CPMs all point up. The only hedge is competition: the lane rewards the specific, not the general.

## 8. DOSSIER 06 // No-code & automation

**The signal.** **+220% year-over-year search growth** (OutlierKit, September 2026 — verified publication), and the niche the research calls **the most underserved** — explicitly flagged single-source, lighter evidence. Search growth is the signal here, not channel growth: people are looking for this faster than creators are making it.

**The pay.** **$10–25 CPM** creator-reported (OutlierKit — verified publication). The audience is businesses and operators — the buyer set advertisers pay for.

**The competition.** Low-to-underserved (OutlierKit — single-source on the "most underserved" label; the figure itself is verified-publication). Read the label with the flag attached: it's one outlet's assessment, not a measured market.

**What it takes.** Build real automations and document the process — this niche is proof-of-work. The entry kit is a screen recorder and actual client or personal workflows to automate. Lilach Bullock's niches-with-room list independently flags **niche B2B explainers** (e.g., small-business tax-change walkthroughs) as having room (single-source, creator's own analysis) — the same underserved-B2B thesis from a second angle.

**Who it suits.** Operators, process nerds, and freelancers who already automate client work. If your idea of fun is wiring three tools together so a task disappears, this is your format.

**The risks.** Single-source weight on the core claim. And platform risk cuts both ways: no-code tools change their pricing and features constantly, and a channel built on one tool's workflow inherits that tool's business decisions.

**The 2027 read.** Heating, with the single-source caveat in bold. The demand signal (+220%) is verified-publication; the "most underserved" label is not. Verify with your own keyword research before committing.

## 9. DOSSIER 07 // Gen Z personal finance

**The signal.** **+180% search growth** (OutlierKit, September 2026 — verified publication) and the **highest breakout rate measured in any niche: 16.7% of videos** beat their channel average by 10x (OutlierKit, August 2026 — verified publication). TikTok's own data names personal finance one of its two fastest-growing niches (TikHub — verified publication).

**The pay.** **$18–45 CPM** creator-reported (OutlierKit — verified publication); the broad finance/business band is **$8–35 RPM** creator-reported (OutlierKit, June 2026 — verified publication) against **$1–5 RPM** for gaming and entertainment — roughly a **7x gap per view**. The median check: AIR's 300-channel dataset puts Education & Science at **$10.22 RPM**, the highest measured median (May 2025–May 2026 — verified). Finance sponsors pay **$40–80+ CPM** (UTubeKit — verified publication) — sponsorships are the second layer of this dossier's economics. Brand deals in general pay roughly **3–5x AdSense** ($15–80 per 1K views, AIR agency-benchmark compilation — verified compilation, underlying agencies single-source each).

**The competition.** **High.** This is the dossier's central tension: the best pay and the highest breakout rate, in the most contested lane. The Niche Atlas rates it Stable, not Heating, for exactly this reason.

**What it takes.** Real financial literacy and research depth — the audience is young and skeptical, and wrong advice travels fast. Honest framing: this is educational content, **not financial, legal, or tax advice** — and the disclaimer has to be on the page, in the video, and in your own head. A camera or screen, a spreadsheet, and the discipline to show your sources.

**Who it suits.** Disciplined researchers with real money experience — people who have actually budgeted, invested, or dug out of debt, and can teach the mechanics without selling a lifestyle. Gen Z creators talking to Gen Z peers get the authenticity premium; everyone else competes on depth.

**The risks.** Competition is the obvious one. The subtle one: **advertiser CPMs are not channel medians** — AIR measured the top 10% of channels taking 58% of ad revenue *within the same niches* (300-channel dataset — verified). Your niche's $45 CPM ceiling is not your floor. One seasonal note the research flags: finance and tax channels often see RPM *rises* in Q1 while the rest of the market falls (Influencer Marketing Hub — single-source) — seasonality isn't universal, and it's an advantage here.

**The 2027 read.** Stable — the pay is real, the breakout rate is the best measured, and the competition is the price of admission. The Atlas rates it Stable: works, but nothing is newly favoring it beyond the Q1 seasonal edge.

## 10. DOSSIER 08 // Cybersecurity & privacy

**The signal.** **+160% search growth** (OutlierKit, September 2026 — verified publication), with **thin competition** (OutlierKit — single-source).

**The pay.** **$12–28 CPM** creator-reported (OutlierKit — verified publication). The buyer set — security vendors, VPNs, password managers, B2B software — is exactly the high-intent audience advertisers overpay for.

**The competition.** Thin (OutlierKit — single-source). The Atlas rates it Heating on this basis with the flag attached.

**What it takes.** Real technical knowledge — this is a credibility niche, and the audience can spot a script-reader. Demo-based content: show the attack, show the fix, show the tool. A lab environment (even a virtual machine on your own computer), a screen recorder, and the accuracy to never teach something that gets someone hacked.

**Who it suits.** The security-curious, IT professionals, and privacy advocates. If you read breach write-ups for fun, the audience is already yours.

**The risks.** Single-source weight on the competition and figure claims. High-stakes wrongness: a wrong tutorial here costs someone their data. And the advertiser base is concentrated — if the security-software marketing cycle cools, so does the CPM.

**The 2027 read.** Heating — +160% search growth at thin competition is the classic underserved shape. Verify the competition claim yourself (search the niche filtered by "this month" and count the channels under 10K subscribers — Lilach Bullock's method, single-source) before you commit.

## 11. DOSSIER 09 // Legal & tax education

**The signal.** Named **the most underserved high-payer** in the research (UTubeKit, July 2026 — verified publication). The growth signal here isn't a multiple — it's the gap between pay and competition.

**The pay.** **$15–40 CPM** creator-reported (Famety, September 2026 — verified publication); legal separately at **$10–35 CPM** (upGrowth, September 2026 — verified publication). Legal and insurance sit near the top of every 2026 CPM ranking the research checked.

**The competition.** Low — that's the entire thesis. High pay plus low competition is the rarest combination on this page, and it exists here because the entry requirement is a credential.

**What it takes.** Real legal or tax knowledge — the moat is professional credibility. Paralegals, accountants, tax preparers, enrolled agents: the people who already answer these questions for a living. The content is explainer-driven: "what this new rule means for you," "the form everyone files wrong," "the deadline calendar." A camera, a whiteboard or slides, and the discipline to cite the actual statute or form.

**Who it suits.** Legal and tax professionals who can translate jargon into plain language. The audience is small-business owners and freelancers — people with money questions and no lawyer on retainer.

**The risks.** High-stakes wrongness again, with legal exposure on top: this is educational content, **not legal or tax advice**, and the disclaimer is load-bearing, not decorative. Laws change by jurisdiction and by year — a 2025 video about 2026 tax rules is a liability, so the catalog needs maintenance. Advertiser sensitivity exists around anything adjacent to disputes or enforcement.

**The 2027 read.** Heating — underserved high-payers don't stay underserved forever, but the credential moat slows the influx. The Atlas rates it Heating with the same basis.

## 12. DOSSIER 10 // Story narration & audio dramas

**The signal.** **$12.82 RPM** reported (UTubeKit, July 2026 — verified publication, creator-reported range). No breakout multiple in the research — this dossier's signal is the rate, not the velocity.

**The pay.** **$12.82 RPM**, creator-reported (UTubeKit — verified publication). Read it as a range with a wide error bar: it's one outlet's reported figure, and narration rates vary hard by audience geography and watch time.

**The competition.** Medium — and the enforcement environment is the real gate. YouTube's January 2026 inauthentic-content wave removed 16 major channels with 4.7 billion combined views and demonetized thousands of low-effort AI channels (UTubeKit — verified publication). Story narration sits exactly on the line this enforcement draws: **original production is monetizable; reused content is not.**

**What it takes.** A narration voice, original scripts or properly licensed/public-domain source material, and real audio production. Lilach Bullock's niches-with-room list independently flags **long-form audio dramas** as having room — with the stated requirement of real production (single-source, creator's own analysis). A microphone, a DAW, and the patience to record a 40-minute episode cleanly.

**Who it suits.** Voice actors, writers, and audio dramatists. Anyone who has ever been told they should do audiobooks — this is the format that pays for that voice without a publisher.

**The risks.** Rights are the structural risk: narrating copyrighted stories without transformation is reused content, and the January-2026 wave is the precedent. Stick to original work, public domain, or genuinely transformative commentary — and note this is not legal advice. Templated top-5/list videos and article readings are explicitly named as reused content (Synthesys — single-source).

**The 2027 read.** Stable — $12.82 RPM against an enforcement regime that rewards originality. The moat is production quality and original writing; the volume farms are gone.

## 13. The microdrama money map

The format from Dossier 01 deserves its own economics section, because the platform money and the creator money tell different stories.

**The platform is building the rails.** YouTube's September 2026 Shorts seasons-and-episodes feature is first-class support for serialized vertical drama (AIR — verified outlet). TikTok has promoted episodic series since June 2026 (buyTikTokAccount — single-source). YouTube's 2027 priorities include micro-dramas (Lia Haberman's read — single-source). A new Swiss platform, naoo AG, was launching microdramas in October 2026 (research round 7 — single-source) — new entrants keep arriving.

**The operator money is real.** ReelShort's trajectory: **$97M revenue (2023) → $785M (2025) → $1.05B projected 2026**, crossing into its first profit year at roughly **$40M net** (Media Partners Asia, August 2026 — verified across 4+ outlets repeating the figures). Note the denominator discipline from the research: Appfigures' **$1.2B** figure for ReelShort 2025 is *consumer spending* (gross in-app), while MPA's **$785M** is *estimated revenue* — different denominators, both labeled (TheWrap via research round 6 — verified). The market outside China is projected at **$3.6B → $9.5B by 2031** (MPA — forecasts, not audited). One industry estimate puts 2026 microdrama revenue at ~$14B (via FastCompany — single-source, flag it).

**The creator economics are brutal.** On Douyin, 221,900 AI/animated dramas released in H1 2026 drew 515.7 billion views — and **1 in 77 recouped its investment; 98.7% failed to break even** (The Paper, ~October 5, 2026 — single-source). DataEye's VP: "AI is not lowering costs so much as lowering the threshold for losing money." The AI-production ramp is the mechanism of the glut: 0 of 16 new tracked dramas were AI-made in January 2026; **201 of 221** were by September (GASP — single-source, public/auditable dataset). ReelShort's own CEO said that if he were purely a businessman he would stop live-action production today, because the AI response from the market is better than live-action (research round 6 — single-source) — which tells you where the *operators'* margins are, not where a new creator's advantage is.

**The lesson for the dossier.** The platform rails and the operator profits say the format has a future; the 1-in-77 recoupment rate says the future belongs to originals, not volume. Bet on the format if you can produce something a render farm can't: a voice, a cast, a story people finish. The supply glut is the filter, not the opportunity.

## 14. The TikTok lane

TikTok's niche numbers run on a different currency — Creator Rewards, not AdSense — so the dossiers above don't translate directly. The October 2026 research on TikTok:

**What's growing.** The two fastest-growing TikTok niches are **AI & tech** and **personal finance**, both at low competition relative to fitness and beauty (TikHub — verified publication). Mini dramas are TikTok's fastest-growing *new* category, with the platform promoting episodic series since June 2026 (buyTikTokAccount — single-source). Raw US watch-time leaders are still entertainment/comedy, food, beauty, fitness, and lifestyle vlogs — watch time is not the same as growth.

**What it pays.** Business-content CPMs run **$9–18** (TikHub — verified publication). Creator Rewards payouts: US top-tier creators report **$0.40–$1.00 per 1K qualified views**; UK **$0.03–$0.15**; non-premium countries **$0.02–$0.06** (AIR Media-Tech, October 2026 — verified). The qualifiers matter: videos must be over 1 minute, a qualified view means 5+ seconds watched, and qualifying views run roughly **half** of raw views (AIR — verified). Entertainment — the biggest watch-time category — pays the lowest: **$0.30–$0.70 RPM** reported (buyTikTokAccount — single-source). Brand deals pay **10–50x the Creator Fund** (InfluenceFlow — single-source) — on TikTok, the audience is the product and the deal is the revenue.

**The reach environment.** Metricool's 2026 TikTok study (2.3M posts, 92K+ accounts — single-source) measured video reach **down 28.73% year over year** and views down 31.30%, with engagement holding at 4.20% (+9% YoY) and 72.70% of views still coming from the For You page. TikTok's US For You feed has run on the Oracle-retrained US-only model since April 2026 (SocialPilot — single-source); creators reported "reach swings" during recalibration, but no measured data attributes the reach decline to retraining vs. competition. Read the reach decline as the environment, not as a verdict on any niche.

**Getting paid faster.** TikTok One Pay — announced at the TikTok Creators Summit (Tubefilter, September 29, 2026 — single-source) — pays creators for brand deals through the TikTok One marketplace with payment landing **under 30 days** after posting (vs the industry-standard net 60/90), with TikTok taking **zero cut**. No creator payout reports existed as of October 11, 2026 — the first sub-30-day cycle lands in late October. Watch this space; cash flow is a feature.

**Underserved on TikTok** (InfluenceFlow — single-source): sustainable/green living, mental health, tech repair and right-to-repair, blue-collar careers, senior lifestyle, and niche hobbies.

**What TikTok removes or buries.** Avoid as categories: gambling and crypto (documented removal risk), medical-advice claims, reposted clips with little commentary (flagged unoriginal), and pure news/politics (ineligible for the For You feed) — all via buyTikTokAccount (single-source). TikTok's October 8 AI-transparency push adds the compliance layer: a global in-app AI Hub, AI-spam detection in testing, and **3 billion+ videos labeled as AI-generated** (TikTok newsroom — platform-stated).

## 16. The avoid list — how formats die

Not "saturated" — *structurally* broken in late 2026. Each entry is the failure mechanism, because knowing *why* a format dies is what keeps you from building a variant of it. (The Niche Atlas carries the 2027-updated table; this is the mechanics layer.)

**1. Clipping / repost channels.** The October 1, 2026 Shorts original-content update targets exactly this: Shorts recommendations now downrank channels that mainly re-upload others' videos without significant changes — "voiceover describing the clip," minor technical edits, and template bulk changes explicitly **don't count** as original (verified across 6+ outlets: Digital Trends, ppc.land, Storyboard18, NeoTeo). Eleven days in, **zero creator-reported before/after view data exists** — the impact is unmeasured, not absent; the research keeps a late-October/November re-search slot. Creator educator Rene Ritchie's note: the recommendation system will *re-evaluate reach* for affected channels (verified per the October research rounds). The scale of what's being squeezed: one streamer reportedly paid clippers **$1.4M in five weeks** (Clownfish TV — single-source), and clipping payments run **KSh 130–520 per 1K views** (Kenyan Mirror, October 5, 2026 — single-source). Clipping survives only as **commentary and analysis**, not re-upload — and no "shadowban checker" can tell you where you stand, because no Studio surface exists for it (panstag's warning — verified per the research rounds).

**2. Low-effort AI slop.** YouTube's January 2026 inauthentic-content enforcement removed **16 major channels (4.7 billion combined views)** and demonetized thousands of low-effort AI channels (UTubeKit — verified publication). The rule that remains: faceless still works, but **only with visible human creativity** — original audio, real production, a human making decisions. The risky faceless formats, named: AI personas giving money or health advice (unmonetizable), article readings, clip compilations, and templated top-5/list videos — all counted as reused content (Synthesys — single-source).

**3. The kids-content revenue trap.** Fastest growth on the board (1.59%/mo), worst pay ($0.33 RPM — about 3 million views per $1,000), plus strict ad rules on children's content (AIR datasets — verified). A red flag **if revenue matters**; a fine lane if the goal is audience, not income. The 2027 kicker: Shorts-heavy kids channels also face the February-2027 10M-views/90-day Shorts revenue floor (AuditSocials — verified multi-source).

**4. Shorts-only channels chasing pool revenue.** Shorts return **3–14% of the same channel's long-form RPM** in almost every niche (AIR, 274 channels — verified), with most niches at $0.02–$0.20 per 1K views. With the February-2027 floor, a Shorts-only channel with no long-form catalog is building on the thinnest revenue layer there is.

**5. TikTok's buried categories.** Gambling and crypto (removal risk), medical-advice claims, reposted clips with little commentary, and pure news/politics (For You-ineligible) — buyTikTokAccount (single-source). These aren't competitive disadvantages; they're distribution zeros.
