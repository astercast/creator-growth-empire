# The Metrics Glossary

> Every metric that matters, what it actually means, what "good" looks like as creator-reported ranges — and which numbers to ignore until they matter. Companion to the Diagnose My Video tool.

**The honesty banner, up front.** Every range on this page is a *rule of thumb from creator-reported ranges* — the numbers creators share with each other, plus the thresholds already published in this site's own chapters. They are **not platform-published benchmarks**: YouTube, TikTok, and Meta do not publish "good CTR" tables, and anyone selling you one is guessing. Your own channel average is always the real baseline. Compare every video against *your* last ten, not someone's screenshot.

## CTR — click-through rate

**What it is:** the percentage of impressions that became views. Someone saw the package; did they click?

**What "good" looks like (creator-reported rule of thumb):** below ~2% is a packaging problem; 4–10% is healthy. This is the threshold [Phase 2](phase-2-youtube-mastery.html) already teaches — repeated here, not invented here.

**The context that changes everything:**

- A video shown to a broad cold audience (browse) always CTRs lower than one shown to subscribers. Same video, different audience, different CTR.
- Search/intent videos live at lower CTRs happily — the click comes from intent, not curiosity.
- New videos sit inside a 48-hour test: don't touch the packaging until it's over. Changing the title or thumbnail resets the test.

**When it lies:** high CTR with terrible retention means the packaging overpromised. The click was real; the video wasn't. Fix the video, not the thumbnail.

## AVD — average view duration

**What it is:** how long people actually watch, in minutes and seconds. The single best content-quality signal the site teaches.

**What "good" looks like:** there is no universal number — a 4-minute AVD is elite on a 5-minute video and catastrophic on a 40-minute one. The rule from [Phase 2](phase-2-youtube-mastery.html): compare against *your own* average, not someone else's. Rising AVD video-over-video beats any absolute number.

**The context that changes everything:**

- AVD is an average — the retention *curve* tells you where the average came from. Two videos with identical AVD can have completely different problems.
- Absolute minutes matter more than the percentage for ad revenue; the percentage matters more for the algorithm's satisfaction read. Track both, optimize the curve.

**When it lies:** a high AVD on a video nobody clicked is a packaging problem wearing a quality costume. Check CTR first.

## Retention curves

**What it is:** the percentage of viewers still watching at each point in the video — the graph, not the average.

**What "good" looks like:** there is no good *shape* in the abstract — there is only your shape getting better. The chapter-standard reads, straight from [Phase 2's retention repair](phase-2-youtube-mastery.html):

- **Sharp early drop** → hook problem.
- **Gradual decline** → pacing problem.
- **Cliff at one timestamp** → that specific moment failed. Go watch it.

**The context that changes everything:** the first 30 seconds decide most videos' fates. A drop in the first 30 seconds means the packaging promised something the opening didn't deliver instantly — the fix is mechanical: move the payoff moment to second 5.

## Traffic sources

**What it is:** where your views come from — browse (Home/Suggested), search, Shorts feed, external, channel pages, and the rest.

**What "good" looks like:** not a number — a *mix that matches your strategy*:

- **Browse-heavy** → you're winning the curiosity game. Double down on packaging; this is where outliers are born. Volatile, huge upside.
- **Search-heavy** → you're winning the intent game. Build the library; a good tutorial earns for *years*. Slow, compounding, nearly immortal.
- **Shorts-feed-heavy** → you're winning the swipe game. Different business (see below).

[Phase 2](phase-2-youtube-mastery.html)'s standing advice: new channels should play **both** — searchable evergreen as the foundation (the baseline), browse-aimed videos as the lottery tickets (the spikes). Over time your analytics tell you which game your audience prefers. Then lean in.

**When it lies:** a traffic-source pie chart never tells you what to make next — it tells you which game you're already winning. The monthly ritual (rank last month by views-per-subscriber *and* by retention; the overlap is your format) tells you what to make.

**Read next:** [Phase 2 — YouTube Mastery](phase-2-youtube-mastery.html)

## Returning vs new viewers

**What it is:** of the people who watched, how many had seen you before.

**What "good" looks like (creator-reported pattern, not a benchmark):** a healthy *growing* channel converts new viewers into returning ones. The two failure modes, from [Phase 2](phase-2-youtube-mastery.html):

- **All-new, no returning** → entertainment without loyalty. People watch; nobody stays.
- **All-returning, no new** → preaching to the choir. The room loves you; the room isn't growing.

**The context that changes everything:** this is the metric behind "great retention, no subscribers" — the audience is renting, not buying. The fix is structural (series formats, a channel promise), not cosmetic. Run it through [Diagnose My Video](video-doctor.html).

## Shorts feed vs browse

**What it is:** two different discovery games that happen to share an app.

- **Shorts feed** is swipe-driven. Per the October 2026 research, completion and watch time dominate, with rewatches, shares, and saves next; likes are a declining signal. The metric that matters is **completion rate**, not CTR — nobody clicks, they just don't swipe.
- **Browse** is click-driven. CTR × retention. Packaging decides the click; the video decides the watch.

**What "good" looks like:** on Shorts, completion rate trending up video-over-video. On browse, the CTR rules above. Never compare a Short's numbers to a long video's — different games, different scoreboards.

**The context that changes everything:** they're different businesses sharing a channel ([Phase 2](phase-2-youtube-mastery.html)). Shorts want punchy, loopable, high-retention; long-form wins on packaging plus a satisfying watch. A Shorts audience does not auto-convert to long-form — the bridge (30-second trailer within 24 hours, pinned comments) has to be built deliberately.

## What NOT to watch (yet)

From [Phase 2's don't-matter list](phase-2-youtube-mastery.html) — the numbers that eat beginners' attention and give nothing back:

- **Subscriber count, day to day.** A lagging indicator. [Phase 1](phase-1-content-engine.html) is explicit: track ideas banked, videos shipped, and average retention — not subscribers.
- **Likes.** A weak signal everywhere in 2026.
- **Comment volume without sentiment.** Ten thoughtful comments beat two hundred emoji.
- **Any single video's first 24 hours.** The test isn't over. Let the data come in.

## Stage-appropriate metrics

Not every metric matters at every stage. Watching the wrong number early is how beginners burn out — [the Money Map](money-map.html) exists to keep your attention on the right job. The mapping:

| Money Map stage | Watch this | Ignore this (for now) |
|---|---|---|
| 0 — The Start Line | Videos shipped, retention trend | CTR, subscriber count |
| 1 — First $100 | CTR + retention (packaging is the lever) | RPM, sponsor math |
| 2 — The Engine Runs | Traffic sources, returning vs new | Team P&L |
| 3 — The Business | RPM, revenue per format | Vanity reach |
| 4 — The Company | Cohort retention, team P&L (the Simulator) | Any single video |

The beginner rule, stated plainly: **don't obsess over CTR before you have 30 videos.** At Stage 0 the engine metrics are ideas banked, videos shipped, and average retention — output first, numbers second. Find your stage on [the Money Map](money-map.html), then come back and watch the right column.

## Reading your retention curve

Pull up your worst-retained video and find the steepest drop. Five shapes cover nearly every diagnosis — each grounded in [Phase 2's retention repair](phase-2-youtube-mastery.html):

1. **The cliff at the start.** The line falls off a cliff in the first 30 seconds, then coasts. *Read:* hook/packaging failure — the opening didn't deliver the promise instantly. *Fix:* move the payoff moment to second 5.
2. **The sawtooth.** The line zigzags down — little recoveries, then more slide. *Read:* if the teeth bounce back up, your re-hooks are working; if the whole saw grinds downward, it's a pacing problem — long unbroken segments. *Fix:* a pattern interrupt every 30–60 seconds.
3. **The single cliff.** Gentle decline, then one vertical drop mid-video, then gentle again. *Read:* one bad section — a tangent, a slow demo, an off-topic rant. *Fix:* go watch that timestamp; cut the section or compress it to 20%.
4. **The end collapse.** Flat and healthy, then everyone leaves near the end. *Read:* normal — unless the collapse starts before 50%. Viewers leaving after the payoff is fine. *Fix:* move anything that matters (CTA, the door to the next video) before the natural end. If they consistently leave at 40%, the video is 40% too long.
5. **The flat low line.** Low from the start, no recovery, no teeth. *Read:* topic mismatch — the promise didn't match what the audience wanted. Under 50 videos this is the tuition phase, not a verdict. *Fix:* repackage the same topic three ways before abandoning it; at 50+ videos with honestly fixed packaging, pivot the format or angle.

## Your numbers vs the Simulator's assumptions

The [Empire Simulator](simulator.html) is a model with stated assumptions — including retention curves it labels as its own ("our curve, labeled"), not platform data. That's honest modeling, and it means one thing for you: **read your real retention first, then tune the model.** The assumptions drawer on the Simulator page lists everything the model takes for granted; this glossary teaches you to read the reality those assumptions stand in for. Change the assumptions, change the empire — but ground them in your graph, not a guru's.

## The glossary at a glance

| Metric | One line | "Good" (creator-reported rule of thumb) |
|---|---|---|
| CTR | Impressions that became clicks | Below ~2% = packaging problem; 4–10% healthy |
| AVD | How long people actually watch | Rising vs your own average beats any absolute |
| Retention curve | Viewers still watching at each moment | Shape tells the story: early drop = hook, sawtooth = pacing, cliff = one bad section |
| Traffic sources | Where views come from | Mix matches strategy: browse = packaging game, search = library game |
| Returning vs new | Renters vs buyers | Growing channels convert new into returning |
| Completion rate (shorts) | Viewers who didn't swipe | Trending up video-over-video |
| Subscribers (day-to-day) | Lagging indicator | Ignore until the engine runs |
