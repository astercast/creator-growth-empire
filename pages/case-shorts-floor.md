# The Monthly Floor: Shorts-First Channels Learn to Count to Ten Million

> The February-2027 YPP change that bites hardest isn't the doubled entry bar — it's the recurring one: 10 million qualified Shorts views per 90 days, checked monthly, to keep earning Shorts ad and Premium revenue. Miss it and Shorts pay stops for that month. This is the documented arc of the Shorts-first channels adapting to it — an industry pattern assembled from reporting, not an invented creator's story.

## The arc: one hurdle becomes a subscription

Before February 2027, getting into YPP was the finish line: clear the bar once, keep the status. For Shorts creators the 2027 terms flip it into a subscription: 10 million qualified Shorts views over the trailing 90 days, re-checked every month, to receive the month's share of the Shorts Creator Pool and Shorts-related Premium revenue. Below it, the channel keeps YPP membership and long-form monetization — the Shorts money just doesn't come that month. Cross back over and it resumes (GSMDome, AuditSocials).

The arithmetic of the floor is unforgiving: 10 million views in 90 days means roughly **111,000 qualifying Shorts views every single day** — and a single viral video no longer buys permanent entry. As Eastleigh Voice's Kenya coverage put it: the change rewards "creators who can consistently attract viewers" over occasional spikes. A channel living on one viral month gets one paid month.

YouTube's VP of Creator Product framed the logic in August 2026: Shorts ads should be "meaningful" revenue — and meaningful takes millions of views (Olivia Hart / StartupInsider, Sep 2026, reporting Hanif's Creator Insider line). Below the floor, YouTube promises substitutes rather than revenue: YouTube Shopping bonuses, brand-deal incentives, trend boosts — details still pending (AIR Media-Tech).

The broadcasters heard the message early. Barrett Media (Oct 6, 2026), covering radio stations and digital brands: for most of them, "Shorts is a marketing tool, not a revenue line" — the platform pays the *stations* in discovery while the revenue comes from direct monetization and brand deals. That's the documented adapter's posture: treat Shorts as the top of the funnel, never the paycheck.

## The revenue stack (below vs above the floor)

| Stream | Above the floor | Below the floor |
|---|---|---|
| Shorts Creator Pool (45% creator share) | Flowing monthly | Zero for the month |
| Shorts-related Premium revenue | Flowing monthly | Zero for the month |
| Long-form ads (55% share) + long Premium | Unaffected | Unaffected — the floor only touches Shorts |
| Fan funding, Shopping, Creator Partnerships (70% fan share) | Unaffected | Unaffected — this is the tier that stays |
| Below-floor incentives | — | Shopping bonuses, brand-deal incentives, trend boosts (promised; details pending — AIR) |
| Sponsors / affiliates | The documented adapter's move: revenue that doesn't require a pool | The documented adapter's move |

The structural lesson: the channels that survive the floor are the ones whose stack never depended on it — sponsorships, affiliates, fan funding, products. Ad revenue was always the tip of the iceberg; the 2027 floor just saws the tip off for everyone below 10M. Revenue figures are source-reported — never promises.

## The 3 decisions that mattered

1. **Treat 10M/90d as a monthly subscription, not a graduation.** The documented adapters stopped thinking "get in" and started thinking "stay in": a rolling 90-day window means every quiet month is a missed invoice. Cadence becomes financial infrastructure — the 90-Day Planner's consistency logic, but with money attached.

2. **Move the revenue below the floor, not above it.** The surviving playbook, as documented: sponsors, affiliates, Shopping, memberships — streams with no view floor at all. YouTube itself is pushing below-floor channels toward Shopping and brand deals. The money that can't be switched off is the money to build first.

3. **Keep the long-form engine running.** The floor touches Shorts only. A channel with long-form monetization keeps earning on every standard video regardless. The cheapest insurance against the monthly check is a format that doesn't have one — which is also why "Shorts-only" is now a red-flag lane in the Niche Atlas avoid list.

## What to steal

- **Dailyize the floor.** 10M ÷ 90 days ≈ 111,000 qualifying views/day. A number you can see every morning beats a threshold you check once a quarter. Run the checker's math on your own numbers — then run the Money Map to place yourself.
- **Build the below-floor stack now.** Sponsors, affiliates, Shopping, memberships — every stream that pays with no floor should exist *before* a quiet month, not after one. Phase 5 orders the stack by effort-to-return; the floor adds a new reason to follow that order.
- **If you're already in YPP: calendar the terms.** Accept updated terms in YouTube Studio by January 31, 2027 — without them, affected features stop earning on Feb 1 (AIR). And the new activity test (1,000 hours/365d, 1M Shorts views/90d, or 2 longs / 5 Shorts per 90 days) has a 90-day recovery window — mark it before you need it.

## What NOT to copy

- **Don't chase 20M to "be safe" and neglect the monthly check.** The 20M figure is the *entry* bar for new applicants; the *ongoing* bar is 10M/90d monthly. A channel that enters at 20M and slides to 9M keeps YPP but loses Shorts pay. The ongoing floor is the one that recurs.
- **Don't apply in January to dodge February unless you're ready.** Applications reaching YouTube by 23:59 UTC Jan 31, 2027 are judged at the old 4,000-hour bar (AIR partner support) — but a rejected January application reapplies at the 8,000-hour bar. Rushing a thin application into a review is a two-way door.
- **Don't treat the floor as the whole story.** Partners also face the new activity test and the Jan-31 terms deadline. The floor is the loudest change, not the only one.
