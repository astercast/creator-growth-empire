# Ben's Bites: 7 Figures in 9 Months

> Ben Tossell built a seven-figure AI-education business that taught 10,000+ people — in under nine months, with himself at the center and AI as his co-pilot.

## The arc: 0 → scale

Ben Tossell arrived with an unfair head start: he had founded Makerpad, a no-code education company, and sold it to Zapier in 2021. Instead of retiring into silence, he started the first AI daily newsletter, Ben's Bites, in October 2022 — right as large language models were about to eat the news cycle.

The newsletter grew fast, but the business came from education: teaching professionals how to use AI to save time and automate their work. In less than a year the business crossed seven figures — reportedly making more than his Makerpad exit — with 10,000+ students taught. The team around him stayed tiny: a one-person core with a handful of collaborators (content and publishing help, operations, and his brother building products, including their in-house ads engine, Grizzly Ads).

## The revenue stack

| Stream | Role in the stack | What was reported |
|---|---|---|
| AI courses / education products | Primary growth engine | 10,000+ students taught |
| Newsletter | Audience + acquisition | Daily AI roundup, one of the earliest in the space |
| Newsletter advertising (Grizzly Ads) | Monetized attention | In-house ad platform built by his team |
| Angel investing (Ben's Bites Fund) | Optionality, not core revenue | Reported on the company about page |

The pattern: free daily signal (the newsletter) → paid depth (education products). The newsletter wasn't the business; it was the top of the funnel the business was built under.

## The 3 decisions that mattered

1. **He rode a wave he was already standing in.** Launching the first AI daily newsletter in October 2022 — weeks before ChatGPT launched — meant the biggest story in tech marketed his product for him. Timing isn't everything, but being early in a real category is the closest thing to a cheat code that exists.

2. **He designed the business around his life, not the other way around.** In his own telling, the operating principle was avoiding things he hated doing: AI handled the grind, contractors and a tiny team handled the rest. The constraint — a business that fits one person — became the architecture. Small teams move fast when everything is automated or delegated on purpose.

3. **He monetized expertise, not attention.** Most newsletters sell ads and stop. Ben's Bites turned 10,000+ readers into students — education products with real prices beat ad inventory every time, because transformation is worth more than impressions.

## What to steal

- **Be early in a category, not just early on a platform.** The newsletter mattered because AI was about to explode. Find the wave that hasn't crested yet.
- **Build the business you actually want to run.** "Avoid the things I hate doing" is a strategy: design roles, tools, and hires around your energy, not around what a business is supposed to look like.
- **Sell the outcome, not the attention.** Ads rent eyeballs; education sells transformation. The highest-margin line in this business came from teaching, not publishing.
- **Keep the core tiny, automate the rest.** One decision-maker, AI as co-pilot, targeted hires for the rest. Every employee you don't need is margin you keep.

## What NOT to copy

- **He didn't start from zero.** A startup exit, operator credibility, and a network came first. The "9 months" clock started after years of building the person who could move that fast.
- **The AI-newsletter window of 2022–2023 was a once-a-cycle moment.** Launching "another AI newsletter" today means competing with everyone, including incumbents with distribution. The category timing can't be repeated — find yours instead.
- **"One person" had a small team behind it.** The about page lists content/publishing help, an operations lead, and a builder. Solo in spirit, staffed in practice. Don't run a 7-figure education business with literally no support and expect his results.
- **The figures are self-reported.** Seven figures, 10,000+ students, "more than my exit" — all from Ben's own interviews and the company site, not audited statements.
