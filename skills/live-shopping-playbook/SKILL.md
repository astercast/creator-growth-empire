# Live Shopping Playbook

## Use when
- Planning your first TikTok live selling session (Phase 6 merch stage)
- Turning POD or owned-inventory merch into a repeatable live format
- Deciding whether live selling is worth the effort for your audience size

## Inputs
- Your product line (POD staples or owned inventory) with per-unit margin: $5–$15 on a $25–$35 POD shirt; 40–60% gross on owned inventory (vs 20–30% on POD)
- Audience size and where it lives (TikTok live shopping grew +60% YoY in H1 2026; affiliate engagement 5.2%, 160% above Instagram, the channel has the tailwind)
- Samples in hand, ordered before launch (~20% sample discounts are standard), washed 3x, size-run checked

## Procedure
1. **Validate the unit economics first.** 30–60% margins on POD staples (e.g. Bella + Canvas 3001 base ~$11.92). If the per-unit profit can't survive a flash-sale discount, the live format will move units and lose money. Owned inventory (40–60% margins) is the format's natural fuel; POD is the validation step.
2. **Build the 60-minute run of show.** 1-hour+ lives are the 2026 playbook: open with the hero product on your body (0–10 min), rotate 3–5 products max, one flash sale or giveaway per 15-minute block, close with the bundle offer. One host, one camera angle, product always visible.
3. **Write the three blocks.** Hook block (0–60s: the transformation or the price anchor, "this is the shirt that survives 50 washes"), demo block (wear it, stretch it, wash-story it, live proof beats studio photos), urgency block (flash sale window closes when the live ends; say the timer out loud).
4. **Feed the flywheel.** 7–12 second product-first clips posted 1–5x/day drive live attendance, every live should be teased by 2–3 clips that day and reposted as clips after. The live is the event; the clips are the flyers.
5. **Close the loop.** Own the customer data, Shopify store (or equivalent), not a marketplace buyer list. Track per session: viewers, units sold, conversion rate, profit. If a product can't clear 100 units, kill it, your audience voted.

## Worked example
Creator: 30K TikTok followers, one POD hoodie ($42 retail, $14 profit) + one owned-inventory tee ($32 retail, $18 profit, 56% margin).
- Session plan: Friday 8pm, 75 minutes. Clips: 2 teaser clips Thursday (unboxing the washed sample, the price reveal). Run of show: hoodie on body open (0–10), tee demo + wash story (10–25), flash sale: tee $32→$27 for the live window only (still $13 margin, survives the discount), giveaway draw at minute 60 for attendance.
- Math check: 500 live viewers × 4% conversion × $15 avg profit ≈ **$300/session**, illustrative, not a promise; first sessions usually run lower while the format finds its audience.

## Pitfalls
- Running a live with no samples, what arrives must match what you hold up on camera.
- Discounting below margin, a flash sale that loses money is a paid advertisement for your supplier.
- Letting a marketplace own the buyer list, no retargeting, no repeat sales, no business.
- Staying in POD forever, margins can't fund a company; live selling validates, owned inventory scales.
- Income figures are illustrative, never promises, track YOUR sessions' numbers, not the playbook's.
