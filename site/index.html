  • 27Repeat Sponsor BookThe board's paid deals, held as the re-approach book every other money tool drops: longest since payment first, each brand's entered paid amount set against the current rate card the Rate Card Builder builds for the same deal through the Deal Pipeline, with its provenance printed, plus one copyable book for the whole list. It reads the same board the Deal Pipeline writes and adds no pricing, no thresholds, and no renewal-due date of its own — and it attaches no pitch wording, because no template in the repo describes a pitch to a brand that already paid. An empty board, a board with no paid deals yet, and a demo-only board are different states, and the book says which.→
  • 28Sponsor Income LedgerThe board's paid deals as a month-by-month ledger — the collected money broken open instead of left as one lump total: each paid deal grouped under the month it entered the paid stage, a running cumulative, per-brand totals with each brand's share of collected, and per-platform totals, plus one copyable ledger for the whole record. It reads the same board the Deal Pipeline writes and adds no pricing, no rate card, and no thresholds of its own — its only arithmetic is summing and grouping the entered amounts, and its months are stage-entry months, never claimed clearing or payout dates. An empty board, a board with no paid deals yet, and a demo-only board are different states, and the ledger says which.→
  • 29Receivables RegisterThe board's invoiced deals as one register of money owed — the list behind the Deal Pipeline's invoiced-outstanding lump: every invoiced deal, fresh ones included, longest outstanding first, each with the amount owed, the date it entered the invoiced stage, its days outstanding, and its late-payment script from the Negotiation Script Builder through the board, plus one copyable register for the whole record. It reads the same board the Deal Pipeline writes and adds no pricing, no wording, and no thresholds of its own — it deliberately does not gate on the follow-up thresholds the Follow-Through Queue owns, because a register's job is completeness. An empty board, a board with nothing invoiced yet, and a demo-only board are different states, and the register says which.→
  • 30Brand LedgerEvery brand on the board as one relationship total — the question no stage-filtered tool answers: for each brand, how much business is there in total across the whole board, how much of it has been collected, how much is owed, and how much is still open, and which brands the whole pipeline rides on. Each brand carries its total, its collected (paid-stage) sum, its owed (invoiced-stage) sum, its still-open sum (lead, negotiating, terms set, live), its deal and stage counts, its platforms, and its share of the Deal Pipeline's pipeline value, largest relationship first, plus one copyable ledger for the whole record. It reads the same board the Deal Pipeline writes and adds no pricing, no wording, and no thresholds of its own — its only arithmetic is summing and grouping the entered amounts by brand and stage, and a brand total is a sum of current-stage amounts, never claimed as a relationship history or lifetime value. An empty board and a demo-only board are different states, and the ledger says which.→

Each platform carries its total, its collected (paid-stage) sum, its owed (invoiced-stage) sum, its still-open sum (lead, negotiating, terms set, live), its deal and stage counts, its brands, and its share of the Deal Pipeline's pipeline value, largest total first, plus one copyable ledger for the whole record.
