Glossary

The words the empire runs on.

Affiliate
You earn a commission when viewers buy through your link. Low effort, scales with trust. Disclose every time.
AVD
Average view duration — how long people actually watch. Retention graphs matter more than the average: find the exact moment viewers leave and fix that moment.
Bookkeeping
Tracking every dollar in and out of the creator business. The unglamorous habit that separates businesses from expensive hobbies.
Browse traffic
Views from YouTube's Home and Suggested surfaces. Curiosity-driven, won on packaging and broad appeal: volatile, with huge upside — this is where outliers are born.
Burnout
The #1 killer of creator careers. Systems, batching, and sustainable cadence beat heroic effort every time. The algorithm rewards consistency over years, not intensity over weeks.
Content pillars
3–5 repeatable video formats inside your niche. Pillars turn 'what do I make this week' from an existential crisis into a rotation.
CPM
Cost per mille — what advertisers pay per 1,000 ad impressions. Higher than RPM because the platform takes its cut and not every view shows an ad.
Creator Pool
YouTube Shorts' pooled ad revenue model. Shorts ad money goes into one pool per country, then creators get 45% of their allocated share based on eligible views.
CTR
Click-through rate — impressions that became views. On YouTube, 2–10% is the normal band; the right number depends on how broadly YouTube is testing your video.
Digital product
Something you make once and sell forever — presets, templates, courses, guides. The highest-margin revenue a creator can own.
Evergreen
Content that stays relevant for years (tutorials, explainers). Slow start, compounds forever. The backbone of a durable channel.
Hook
The first 1–3 seconds (short-form) or 30 seconds (long-form) that earn the rest of the watch. State the payoff, open a loop, or show the most interesting frame first.
Lead magnet
A free, genuinely useful thing (checklist, template, guide) you trade for an email address. The bridge from rented audience to owned audience.
LLC
Limited Liability Company — separates your personal assets from your business. Educational info only, not legal advice; rules and costs vary by state/country.
Niche
The specific intersection of topic, audience, and format you own. Narrow enough to be the obvious choice, broad enough to never run out of ideas.
Outlier
A video that dramatically over-performs your channel average — 10x your typical views. Your channel's growth plan is mostly 'make more videos like your outliers, but better packaged.'
Owned audience
People you can reach without an algorithm's permission — email list, Discord, SMS. Platforms rent you attention; owned channels are the only equity.
Packaging
Title + thumbnail (+ hook for short-form). The wrapper around the video that decides whether anyone clicks. Creators consistently find packaging moves the needle more than production quality.
Print on demand
Merch with zero inventory: a third party prints and ships when fans order. Low margin, zero risk — the right first step before your own product line.
Qualified views
Views that actually count toward monetization thresholds and payouts — public, eligible-country, policy-compliant, not from bots or ad campaigns. Your Analytics total is always higher than your qualified count.
Repurposing
One idea, five platforms: a long-form video becomes Shorts, TikToks, Reels, a Facebook cut, and a community post. Same research, five chances to win.
Retention
The percentage of viewers still watching at each point in the video. The first 30 seconds decide most videos' fates.
Returning viewers
Viewers who have watched you before, vs new viewers seeing you for the first time. A healthy growing channel converts new viewers into returning ones: all-new with no returning is entertainment without loyalty; all-returning with no new is preaching to the choir.
RPM
Revenue per mille — dollars earned per 1,000 monetized views after the platform's cut. The number that actually lands in your account. Always ask for RPM, never CPM, when someone quotes earnings.
Shorts feed
The swipe-driven discovery surface for YouTube Shorts. Completion and watch time dominate; the metric that matters is completion rate, not CTR — nobody clicks, they just don't swipe.
SOP
Standard operating procedure — the documented 'how we do this' for every repeatable task. SOPs are what let you hire without chaos.
Sponsorship
A brand pays you to feature their product. Priced on audience trust and niche value, not just view counts. Always disclose — it's the law in most countries.
Traffic sources
Where views come from: browse (Home/Suggested), search, Shorts feed, external, channel pages. Browse-heavy means you're winning the curiosity/packaging game; search-heavy means you're winning the intent/library game. New channels should play both.
Trend-jacking
Riding a trend in your niche's style. Fast views, short half-life. Use for discovery spikes, not as the foundation.
YPP
YouTube Partner Program. Tier 1 (500 subs): fan funding. Tier 2 (1,000 subs): full ad revenue. Verify current thresholds live — they changed in 2026 and rise again in 2027 for new applicants.
