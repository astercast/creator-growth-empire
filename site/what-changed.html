  • YouTube Watch With expands to long-form videos (announced Sept 23): reactors keep the live ad money, originals get credit, not a cut

    2026-10-13
    YouTubeMonetizationVerified

    Announced September 23, 2026 at Made on YouTube and in YouTube's own Live post the same day: Watch With, until now piloted on major live broadcasts and tested between creators' livestreams, is expanding to all eligible long-form videos. A creator taps 'React live' on an eligible video and streams their reaction alongside it. The money split is the term that matters: the reacting creator keeps the live ad revenue and any fan funding the reaction stream earns, and the original creator is not paid a share of it. What the original creator gets instead is on-screen attribution, an 'added view credit' in YouTube Studio. YouTube has not said whether that credit ever converts to revenue, and control over which of their videos can be reacted to. YouTube has not published what makes a video eligible, or whether a catalog starts opted in or out. The rollout begins later in 2026 and no start date has been published, so effective_at stays null rather than borrow the announcement day.

  • Kick rewrote Partner Program pay math in September: rolling rate replaces per-stream rate, September errors repaid

    2026-10-13
    KickMonetizationVerified

    In September 2026, Kick Partner Program (KPP) creators reported payouts lower than expected and rates swinging sharply between streams. Kick's director Ethan Wright said the shortfall traced to a manual calculation error: Kick corrected the affected calculations and issued backpay to impacted partners through Stripe, the backpay shows in a creator's Stripe dashboard, not in Kick's on-site earnings interface. The rate model changed at the same time: KPP rates were calculated stream by stream, and are now calculated on a rolling basis over a stretch of recent broadcasts including the current one, so a single quiet stream moves the rate less. Kick says the revised method is also meant to make view botting and tab manipulation harder to profit from. The 95/5 subscription split and KICKs tipping are unchanged, and neither report publishes a numeric KPP rate or a calendar day in September for the change, the dates below stay null rather than borrow one.

  • X moved U.S. creator payouts from Stripe to X Money on Sept 2, now the only route for U.S. payouts

    2026-10-13
    XMonetizationVerified

    X announced on September 2, 2026 that all U.S. payouts from Original Content Rewards and creator Subscriptions are paid through X Money, its own payments service, instead of Stripe, and confirmed to TechCrunch that the switch is mandatory for U.S. creators, with no other payout option offered. Creators outside the U.S. stay on Stripe. The practical change is timing and custody: a payout is available the moment it is sent, replacing the old Stripe run every two weeks behind a $30 minimum, but the money now lands inside X's own financial product first. X Money is not a bank; its accounts are held at FDIC-insured Cross River Bank. U.S. creators who have not opened X Money need to set it up or their next payout has nowhere to go.

  • Facebook's two-link-post test expanded in September: more Pages capped unless they pay for Meta One

    2026-10-13
    FacebookPolicyVerified

    Meta has run this as a test since December 2025, and in September 2026, as Meta One for Business launched (announced September 15), managers at more Pages and professional-mode accounts began hitting the cap: two organic posts with external links per calendar month for free, with anything past that shown as plain text rather than a clickable link. Social Media Today reported the expansion on September 17; Press Gazette reported on October 7 that news-publisher Pages are excluded for now, and priced the tiers that lift the cap: $49.99 a month for 8 link posts, $149.99 for 20, $499.99 for unlimited, while the $14.99 Essential tier adds none.   • Kenya's 5% withholding now comes off platform payouts. Meta from January, YouTube from September earnings

    2026-10-13
    OtherLegalVerified

    The 5% is not a new platform fee: it is Kenya's withholding tax on digital-content income (in law since the Finance Act 2023), and 2026 is the year the platforms began deducting it at source. Meta went first, creators were told in November 2025 that 5% would come off Facebook and Instagram payouts from January 1, 2026, shown on the monthly remittance advice. Google followed for YouTube: 5% off finalised AdSense earnings, starting with September 2026 earnings paid in October, with a verified Kenya Revenue Authority PIN due in AdSense by October 1 or payments are held. TikTok asked Kenyan creators for tax information in mid-September, but has published no rate, start date, or consequence, do not assume the 5% figure or the YouTube deadlines are TikTok's. The withheld amount is an advance against your own tax bill, credited through a withholding certificate, not a second tax on the same income.

  • Meta banned TikTok and ByteDance ads on Facebook and Instagram in seven countries (Oct 8)

    2026-10-12
    FacebookPolicyVerified

    From October 8, Meta no longer sells ads or paid marketing messages to ByteDance on Facebook, Instagram, and Threads in the United States, Canada, Egypt, Indonesia, Japan, Thailand, and Vietnam, and the ban reaches third-party advertisers whose campaigns link out to TikTok or other ByteDance properties in those markets. Organic posts and mentions are not covered; this is a paid-promotion rule. Meta's stated reason is that it does not have to run ads for a competitor whose goal is to pull people off its apps. For creators and brands, a paid Meta campaign can no longer be the bridge that sends viewers to a TikTok account in those seven markets, check any cross-platform campaign's destination before it is rejected at review.

  • Twitch opened Creator Sponsorships to Affiliates on Aug 20, 2026

    2026-10-12
    TwitchMonetizationVerified

    Brand campaigns run through Twitch used to be a mostly Partner-only door. From August 20, Affiliates worldwide can be found by brands, invited to campaigns, and paid through the Sponsorship Dashboard in the Creator Dashboard, the same campaign pool Partners use, not a smaller Affiliate pool.   • TikTok Community Guidelines 2026H2 (effective Sep 24): bans can reach associated accounts

    2026-10-12
    TikTokPolicySingle-source

    Released August 25 and in force from September 24, the new guidelines leave most content rules where they were and rewrite the enforcement side. The penalty ladder is now spelled out, warning, temporary feature or account restriction, full ban, and account status (Healthy, Fair, Severe, Banned) reflects the last 90 days, with violations expiring after 90 days and no published line between statuses. Two parts are new: optional policy training can lift eligible restrictions for certain first violations (severe violations do not qualify), and a serious violation or an attempt to dodge enforcement can now reach associated accounts, a term TikTok does not define. Undisclosed ads are no longer automatically kept off For You in this text, though other TikTok pages still say they can be pulled.

  • X retired Revenue Sharing on Sept 7; Original Content Rewards pays only for qualified impressions

    2026-10-12
    XMonetizationVerified

    X stopped new Revenue Sharing enrollments on August 7, 2026, retired the program on September 7, and opened applications for Original Content Rewards from September 8. The replacement pays on qualified impressions only: views by Premium subscribers on the Home Timeline with at least half the post visible; paid, promoted, and artificial impressions do not count. Gates: an active Premium, Premium+, or Premium Business subscription, 500 verified followers, and 500,000 verified Home Timeline impressions in the prior 90 days. Copied, aggregated, and lightly edited posts do not qualify, and X has published no per-impression rate, rates quoted from the old program describe a dead program.

  • Facebook Content Monetization: the March 2026 originality rule can cut reach, then pay

    2026-10-12
    FacebookPolicyVerified

    Meta ended In-Stream Ads, Ads on Reels, and the Performance Bonus on August 31, 2025 and merged them into one Content Monetization program. In March 2026 it tightened the other side of the deal: content that is duplicative, or that makes only minor edits to someone else's post, borders, captions, speed changes, a logo on a repost, is treated as unoriginal, deprioritized in distribution, and a page that keeps posting mainly unoriginal work can be marked non-recommendable and demonetized. Cross-posting your own Reels is fine. Creator guides report the gate as 10,000 followers, 600,000 eligible minutes viewed in 60 days, and 5+ published videos, and only a share of raw views ever counts as eligible plays, so plan on eligible plays, not views.
