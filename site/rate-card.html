Some reports run lower at the bottom ends (e.g. nano IG $10–$100 per projectcasting). Treat the low end as "small brands, simple deliverables" and the high end as "proven engagement, competitive niche."

Platform multipliers (at equal follower counts)

YouTube costs 1.7x Instagram. TikTok costs 0.85x Instagram. (Elev8or 2026, micro creators, lifestyle baseline: e.g. a $250–$1,500 IG feed post maps to $425–$2,550 on YouTube, $213–$1,275 on TikTok.) LinkedIn runs 1.4x, Snapchat 0.7x, X 0.6x.

Per-follower rates fall as accounts grow: nano runs ~$25–$50 per 1K followers; macro runs ~$10–$12 per 1K. Follower count is the weakest pricing variable — engagement and expected views are what you're actually selling.

Sponsored CPMs (per 1,000 views)

  • YouTube integrated mid-roll slot: $20–$35 (dedicated video: $50–$90)
  • YouTube general: $15–$40
  • Instagram sponsored Reel (median): $22.40 CPM
  • Instagram/TikTok general: $10–$30
  • By YouTube category: finance/real estate ~$58, B2B SaaS & AI ~$46.50, health/fitness ~$28, beauty/fashion ~$24.50, gaming/tech ~$18.50, entertainment/comedy ~$14

The context that matters: a YouTube integrated slot pays 9–15x the median ad RPM (~$2.30). For mid-size creators, sponsors are typically 50–80% of income. That's why pricing this right is the highest-leverage negotiation of your year.

What creators actually quote (Sep 2026, reported)

Tier tables are compiled ranges. This is the other lens: measured asking prices. In September 2026, Cheerful analyzed 905 creator asks, 298 brand offers, and 2,356 itemized rate-card lines from real brand–creator email threads (reported via Talentir's 2026 influencer-rates roundup). The typical quoted prices: $1,800 for an Instagram Reel, $2,000 for a TikTok video, $3,000 for a video integration, $500 for UGC without posting. Typical asks by size: $450 at 1K–10K followers, $4,250 at 250K+.

Two cautions before you anchor on these. First, they are quotes — what creators ask, not what deals close at, so the ~23% listed-vs-accepted gap below still applies. Second, a format median flattens tier and niche: a finance integration and a comedy integration are both "video integrations" here and are not worth the same money. Use the medians to sanity-check a tier-table quote, not to replace it.

The modeled cross-check (labeled as such): Click Analytic's Creator Pricing Report 2026 models one sponsored Instagram feed post from 5.9 million accounts (a full-year 2025 snapshot, published as the 2026 edition): nano (10K–50K) $114–$679, micro (50K–100K) $899–$1,500, mid-tier (100K–500K) $2,600–$5,700, macro (500K–1M) $5,900–$12,200, mega (1M+) from $12,200. Its tiers are cut differently from the table above — compare shapes, not labels — and the source itself calls these modeled planning benchmarks, not guaranteed quotes. One finding worth memorizing: only 14.6% of those 5.9 million accounts had both good audience quality and at least 2% engagement. The top of any range is a quality price, not a follower price.

Both blocks live in the machine-readable card as sponsorship_quoted_prices_2026 (calculators v1.5.0), bases labeled, and the Deal Room checks every logged offer against them.

The negotiation data

  • Creators list rates ~23% above what they accept (28% gap for nano) — so quote high and expect to be negotiated down.
  • 41% offer package discounts on 3+ posts — build this into your bundles, don't let brands discover it.
  • Video commands a 20–40% premium over static formats.
  • Multi-platform bundles add 30–50% — selling the same concept across YT + TikTok + IG is more than the sum.
  • Macro creators cost ~45% more per authentic engagement than nano — small creators are the value buy, and brands know it.
  • Holiday 2026 note: Black Friday brand deals can pay less per post than normal rates — brands buy in bulk during BFCM (Lilach Bullock, Oct 2026). Affiliate commissions are often cut during BFCM week because the brand's margin is already thin from the discount itself. Price holiday packages with the bulk math, not the hope of a seasonal premium.
  • 2026 usage-rights benchmarks: paid-ad usage for up to 3 months adds 25–50% to the base rate; 3–6 months adds 50–75%; 6–12 months adds 75–100%; perpetual/buyout starts at 2x. Whitelisting (brand running paid ads through your account) runs 30–100% of the base fee per month (projectcasting 2026).

The most-neglected line item: usage rights

When a brand re-runs your $300 video as paid ads for a year, they're capturing thousands in value. Price paid-ad usage separately from the post fee — every time. This is the single most common money left on the table in creator deals. See Phase 5's negotiation section for the full lever list (exclusivity +25%, usage +50–100%, whitelisting premium, bundles −15%).

How to use this card

  1. Find your tier and platform — that's your starting range.
  2. Anchor on expected views (last-10-video average), not followers: $20–$50 per 1,000 expected views is the common starting point (finance/tech 2–3x, entertainment lower).
  3. Quote at the top of the range — you'll be negotiated down ~23%.
  4. Add usage rights as a separate line item. Always.
  5. Re-price every 3 closed deals: raise 20% until your close rate drops to ~1 in 3.
