Stage playbooks

Each stage: the goal, the 3 moves that matter, the trap to avoid, and what "graduate" actually looks like. Stages describe what creators at this level typically focus on — never income promises.

Stage 0 — The Start Line

$0 / MONTH

The goal

Prove you can ship. Creators at this stage typically focus on output volume and finding a format that fits — not on money, which hasn't arrived yet.

The 3 moves that matter

  • The Niche Atlas

    Pick one lane you could fill for 100 videos. Real demand, competition, and revenue bands per niche.

  • Idea Bank

    Capture every idea and score it before you film. The bottleneck is almost always starting, not finishing.

  • 90-Day Planner

    Turn intention into a cadence you can keep. Consistency is the whole game before you have an audience.

The trap

Tool-collecting instead of posting. New gear, new logos, new software — none of it is the work. Thirty rough videos beat one perfect channel trailer. (Phase 0's honesty: the avoid list exists for a reason.)

Graduate when

30 pieces posted AND (first 1,000 subs OR first $1 earned). Output first, numbers second.

Stage 1 — First $100

FIRST $100

The goal

Turn attention into your first real revenue. Creators here typically focus on learning what their audience is worth — usually via ads or affiliates — before chasing sponsors.

The 3 moves that matter

  • Revenue Calculator

    See honest revenue ranges for your current views. Knowing the math stops you from underpricing yourself later.

  • Hook Builder

    The fastest lever at this stage is retention in the first 3 seconds. Five hooks per topic, one per Playbook structure.

  • The Short-Form Playbook

    The serialized formats that convert casual viewers into subscribers — subscribers are what sponsors eventually pay for.

The trap

Sponsor-chasing before you have leverage. Saying yes to a $50 brand deal teaches the market you're cheap. Build the audience; the leverage follows.

Graduate when

$100 in cumulative content revenue — earned, not promised. One lucky month doesn't count; repeatability does.

Stage 2 — The Engine Runs

$1K / MONTH

The goal

Make $1K months repeatable. Creators at this stage typically focus on their first real sponsor relationships and on systematizing production so output doesn't depend on motivation.

The 3 moves that matter

  • The Deal Room

    Manage your first deals end-to-end: offer tracking, usage-rights pricing, negotiation scripts, the deal memo.

  • Batching Planner

    Does your shoot capacity actually cover your cadence? The honest surplus/shortfall math before you promise a sponsor a schedule.

  • Empire Simulator

    Model 12–24 months of growth and revenue before you live them. A model, not a prediction — it shows what has to be true.

The trap

One revenue stream. Ads-only is a single point of failure — one algorithm change, one demonetization wave. Stack a second stream before you need it. (Phase 5: diversify revenue is a survival rule, not a growth hack.)

Graduate when

Three consecutive $1K+ months from at least two revenue streams. Consistency, then diversification.

Stage 3 — The Business

$10K / MONTH

The goal

Treat it like a business, because it is one. Creators at this stage typically focus on their first hire, real contracts, and separating the money from their personal life.

The 3 moves that matter

  • The First Hire

    What to hire for first (almost always editing or admin), how to scope the role, and the mistakes that cost creators their momentum.

  • Hiring Cost Calculator

    The real monthly and annual cost of a hire — and the revenue needed to cover it. Honest math, no promises.

  • Phase 7 — The Business

    LLC, taxes, separating accounts. Educational, not legal or tax advice — but the structure that keeps a $10K/mo operation from eating you.

The trap

Hiring before the math works. A full-time salary on shaky revenue turns a good channel into a stressed company. Contractors first, employees when the calculator says so.

Graduate when

Three consecutive $10K+ months with at least two revenue streams AND your first hire made. Revenue plus leverage.

Stage 4 — The Company

COMPANY

The goal

Build something that runs without your daily input. Creators at this stage typically focus on team P&L, new product lines, and protecting the thing the audience actually loved.

The 3 moves that matter

  • Empire Simulator — team P&L

    Model the full team cost against projected revenue. At company scale the question flips: what can we afford to try?

  • Case Files

    Documented deconstructions — how PTYA scaled a cohort course, how the sleep channels became a network. Proven structures, reported figures.

  • Phase 8 — Scale

    New formats, new platforms, new products. Scale is a portfolio problem now, not a posting problem.

The trap

Scaling what broke you. Growth at all costs dilutes the voice that got you here. The audience loved a person, not a content factory.

Graduate when

The operation runs a full month without your daily input — and revenue doesn't dip. That's the company. This is the capstone, not a finish line.

The honesty policy

The Money Map is a planning aid, not financial, legal, or tax advice. Stages describe what creators at a given revenue level typically focus on — they are not income promises, timelines, or guarantees. Ranges here are illustrative.
